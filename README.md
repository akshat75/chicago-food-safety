Chicago Food Safety: Inspections at Scale

Analysis of ~314K Chicago food-facility inspections (2010–2026) joined with business license records : inspection patterns, pass rates, repeat violations, and enforcement consistency across the city.

Data Source
Chicago Data Portal (https://data.cityofchicago.org/) : Food Inspections and Business Licenses datasets. Raw CSVs aren't included here (424MB + 331MB, over GitHub's limits) : download from the source and place in the project root to reproduce.

Project Structure
- `01_data_cleaning.ipynb` : loads, cleans, and reshapes both datasets; explodes the packed violations field into a normalized child table; writes to `chicago_food_safety.db`
- `02_sql_analysis.ipynb` : SQL queries answering the business questions below
- `chicago_food_safety.db` : generated by notebook 1 (not tracked in git)

Schema
- inspections : one row per inspection
- violations : one row per (inspection, violation code), reshaped from the raw packed string
- licenses : one row per business, filtered to food-specific license types, deduplicated to the latest record

Key Definitions
- Pass rate = (Pass + Pass w/ Conditions) ÷ (Pass + Fail + Pass w/ Conditions); non-inspection outcomes excluded from the denominator.
- Repeat-violation rate = % of businesses with ≥1 Fail that failed again later.

Findings
1. Risk distribution : 74.48% of inspections target Risk 1 (High), vs. 7.76% for Risk 3 (Low). Likely reflects that the high-risk category has more inspections, rather than meaning that most businesses are high-risk.

2. Seasonality + pass rate : Volume is seasonal (~22-24K winter vs. ~27-29K other seasons, summed across all years),but the pass rate stays fairly consistent at 76–80% throughout the year. This points to inspector scheduling, not food safety varying by month.

3. Top chains by Inspection count : Subway (4,275), Dunkin Donuts (2,233), McDonald's (1,559) lead by inspection count. This largely reflects how many branches each chain has; does not necessarily mean worse food safety practices. *Limitation: legal-suffix name variants (e.g., "Potbelly... LLC") aren't fully merged.*

4. Ward running totals : Growth patterns vary by ward; figures only cover businesses with a food-specific license, so institutional facility types are excluded.

5. Top Violators per facility type, according to violation count : Highlights which businesses have the most violations within their respective facility category. 

6. Repeat-violation rate : 50.78% of businesses that failed an inspection went on to fail again. This suggests that a first failure is a meaningful indicator of future violations rather than simply being a one-time event. 

Known Limitations
- Ward analysis excludes institutional facility types (schools, daycares, nursing homes) not licensed as food businesses.
- Business name matching isn't fully deduplicated across legal-entity variants.
