Chicago Food Safety: Inspections at Scale

Analysis of ~314K Chicago food-facility inspections (2010–2026) joined with business license records : inspection patterns, pass rates, repeat violations, and enforcement consistency across the city, followed by a statistical phase testing which factors are associated with inspection failures and violation counts.

Data Source
Chicago Data Portal (https://data.cityofchicago.org/) : Food Inspections and Business Licenses datasets. Raw CSVs aren't included here (424MB + 331MB, over GitHub's limits) : download from the source and place in the project root to reproduce.

Project Structure
- `01_data_cleaning.ipynb` : loads, cleans, and reshapes both datasets; explodes the packed violations field into a normalized child table; writes to `chicago_food_safety.db`
- `02_sql_analysis.ipynb` : SQL queries answering the business questions below
- `03_hypothesis_testing.ipynb` : Phase 2, five hypothesis tests on inspection outcomes and violation counts (chi-square, ANOVA / Kruskal-Wallis, two-proportion z-test, Pearson / Spearman correlation)
- `chicago_food_safety.db` : generated by notebook 1 (not tracked in git)

Schema
- inspections : one row per inspection
- violations : one row per (inspection, violation code), reshaped from the raw packed string
- licenses : one row per business, filtered to food-specific license types, deduplicated to the latest record

Key Definitions
- Pass rate = (Pass + Pass w/ Conditions) ÷ (Pass + Fail + Pass w/ Conditions); non-inspection outcomes excluded from the denominator.
- Repeat-violation rate = % of businesses with ≥1 Fail that failed again later.
- Prior failure (Phase 2) = the same license number has an earlier inspection with result Fail.
- Statistical significance (Phase 2) : with ~270K inspections in the outcome-based tests, almost any difference reaches p < 0.001, so each result below also gives the raw gap (rates or correlation) to show how large the difference actually is.

Findings
1. Risk distribution : 74.48% of inspections target Risk 1 (High), vs. 7.76% for Risk 3 (Low). Likely reflects that the high-risk category has more inspections, rather than meaning that most businesses are high-risk.

2. Seasonality + pass rate : Volume is seasonal (~22-24K winter vs. ~27-29K other seasons, summed across all years),but the pass rate stays fairly consistent at 76–80% throughout the year. This points to inspector scheduling, not food safety varying by month.

3. Top chains by Inspection count : Subway (4,275), Dunkin Donuts (2,233), McDonald's (1,559) lead by inspection count. This largely reflects how many branches each chain has; does not necessarily mean worse food safety practices. *Limitation: legal-suffix name variants (e.g., "Potbelly... LLC") aren't fully merged.*

4. Ward running totals : Growth patterns vary by ward; figures only cover businesses with a food-specific license, so institutional facility types are excluded.

5. Top Violators per facility type, according to violation count : Highlights which businesses have the most violations within their respective facility category. 

6. Repeat-violation rate : 50.78% of businesses that failed an inspection went on to fail again. This is a business-level figure and is inflated by businesses with many inspections having more chances to fail; Phase 2 (finding 11) shows that an individual inspection is not more likely to fail just because the business failed before.

Phase 2 : Statistical Analysis
Overarching question: What factors are associated with inspection failures and higher violation counts among Chicago food establishments? Outcome-based tests use the 269,811 inspections with a Pass, Fail, or Pass w/ Conditions result. All results are associations, not causal claims.

7. H1, Risk category vs. result (chi-square) : The result mix differs by risk category (χ² = 702.87, p < 0.001). Counterintuitively, Risk 3 (Low) inspections fail most often (29.0%), compared with 22.5% for Risk 2 and 21.8% for Risk 1. The risk category appears to drive how often a facility is inspected, not how likely an inspection is to fail.

8. H2, Violation count across facility types (Kruskal-Wallis, six most-inspected facility types, 287,386 inspections) : Violation counts differ significantly across facility types (H = 1,140.43, p < 0.001). Violation counts are right-skewed in every facility type (skewness 1.2 to 1.6), so Kruskal-Wallis is the primary test; a one-way ANOVA agrees (F = 372.27, p < 0.001). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons were not run, so this shows that facility types differ, not which ones differ most.

9. H3, Winter vs. rest-of-year pass rate (two-proportion z-test) : Pass rate is 78.4% in winter vs. 77.4% in the rest of the year (z = 5.26, p < 0.001). Statistically significant, but the gap is about 1 percentage point, and it is slightly higher in winter, consistent with finding 2.

10. H4, Year vs. violations per inspection (correlation, 314,177 inspections) : Pearson r = 0.023 and Spearman ρ = −0.020 (both p < 0.001). The p-values are small only because of the sample size; both correlations are close to zero and opposite in sign, so there is no meaningful trend in violations per inspection over time.

11. H5, Prior failure vs. current result (chi-square) : The result mix differs by prior failure (χ² = 608.86, p < 0.001). Inspections of businesses with a prior failure fail 21.0% of the time, vs. 24.6% for businesses without one, which is the opposite direction from the hypothesis. One untested explanation is that a business's first inspection on record fails more often than later ones; inspection type was not controlled for.

Known Limitations
- Ward analysis excludes institutional facility types (schools, daycares, nursing homes) not licensed as food businesses.
- Business name matching isn't fully deduplicated across legal-entity variants.
- Inspections of the same establishment are not independent, but the tests treat them as if they were, which makes p-values look stronger than they are. The size of each gap matters more than its p-value.
- Inspection type (canvass, complaint, license, re-inspection) is not controlled for in the Phase 2 tests.
- Phase 2 reports significance and raw gaps only; standardized effect sizes and confidence intervals were not computed.
- H2 covers only the six most-inspected facility types and has no post-hoc comparisons.
